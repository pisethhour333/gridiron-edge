Bet types, plainly

Moneyline

Pick who wins. Negative odds show what you risk to win $100; positive odds show what $100 wins.

-150: bet $150 to win $100 · +130: bet $100 to win $130
Point spread

The favorite must win by more than the spread; the underdog covers by losing by less (or winning). Most spreads are priced near -110 on both sides, so you need to win about 52.4% to break even.

Steelers -3.5: they must win by 4 or more
Total (over/under)

Bet on the combined score of both teams going over or under a number. Weather, pace and injuries to offensive stars move totals most.

O/U 44.5: a 24-21 final (45) goes over
Parlay

Several bets tied into one. Every leg must win. Payouts grow fast, but so does the book's edge, because the margin compounds on each leg.

Three -110 legs pay about +596, but hit only ~13% if each is a true coin flip
Teaser

A parlay where you move the spreads in your favor (usually 6 points in football) in exchange for a smaller payout. The classic version crosses the key numbers 3 and 7.

Two-team 6-pt teaser: +7.5 becomes +1.5 underdog → +13.5
Props

Bets on events inside a game, like a player's passing yards or who scores first. Books often have bigger margins on props.

Push

When the result lands exactly on the line, the bet is refunded. Half-point lines (like 3.5) can't push.

Key numbers

NFL games end with a 3- or 7-point margin far more often than other margins, so a half point around 3 or 7 is worth more than one around 5.

NBA bets, plainly

Moneyline

Pick who wins, overtime included. Big NBA favorites can cost -400 or more, so a moneyline parlay of favorites is popular but pays little per leg.

Celtics -250: bet $250 to win $100
Point spread

The most bet NBA market. The favorite must win by more than the spread. NBA margins spread out more evenly than football, so there are no big key numbers, but half points still matter in close games.

Knicks -6.5: they must win by 7 or more
Total (over/under)

Combined points for both teams, usually between 210 and 240. Fast-paced teams, back-to-backs, and missing stars move totals most. Overtime counts.

O/U 226.5: a 115-112 final (227) goes over
Player points, rebounds, assists

The most popular NBA props. Bet a player over or under a line, like 24.5 points. DraftKings also offers milestones, like “25+ points”, which is the same idea paid at fixed odds. The Props tab shows the chance of each.

Over 24.5 points: he needs 25 or more
3-pointers made

How many threes a player makes. Low numbers swing a lot from night to night, so even good shooters miss a 3+ line often.

PRA (points + rebounds + assists)

One line for a player's points, rebounds and assists added together. It smooths out a bad night in one stat, which is why many bettors like it.

25 pts + 9 reb + 6 ast = 40 PRA
Double-double and triple-double

Double-double means 10 or more in two of points, rebounds and assists. Triple-double means all three. Big men who rebound well hit double-doubles most often.

Quarters, halves and first basket

Spreads and totals for just one quarter or half, and bets on who scores first. These are quick but have more luck and bigger book margins.

Same-game parlay

Several bets from one game in one ticket, like a player's points plus the over. They're popular, but the book prices in how the legs are linked, so the margin is usually bigger than a normal parlay.

Rest and injuries

NBA stars sit more often than in football, and teams often confirm who plays only 30 minutes before tip-off. Teams on the second night of a back-to-back tend to play worse. Always check the injury report before betting player props.

How the model works

Each team's points for minus points against gives its strength per game. Early in the season that's pulled toward average, since a few games say little. Home field adds 1.5 points. A backup QB costs 5.5 points, each other key player out costs 1 point, a bye adds 1 and a short week costs 1. The resulting margin turns into a win chance using the typical NFL game-to-game spread of about 13.5 points.

NBA: each team's points scored minus points allowed per game, blended with last season (pulled 40% toward average for roster changes) until about 10 games are played. Home court adds 2.5 points, and a team on the second night of a back-to-back loses 1.5. The margin becomes a win chance using a game-to-game spread of about 12.5 points. Projected totals come from each team's scoring and defense. Player projections use season averages, adjusted for the opponent's defense and rest.
